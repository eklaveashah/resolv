Feed health

Each source reports one of six states: loading, live, stale (last good copy, with its age), snapshot, rate-limited or offline. Snapshot rows are bundled reference data with a visible timestamp; they are never mixed into a live ranking.

Resolution risk

Right thesis, wrong payout: the risk most tools ignore.

Contracts settle on written criteria, and vague criteria can flip a payout even when the thesis was right. On the sample contracts Resolv rates the resolution wording and shrinks fair value toward a coin flip when the language is loose. The full rules text sits on every market detail.

Ambiguity rating

Each sample contract's resolution wording carries a clear, moderate or vague rating, set by reading it. Live contracts are not rated yet, so no rating is invented for them and no discount is applied.

Fair-value haircut

High ambiguity pulls the estimate toward 50¢ and caps confidence, visible as its own line in the attribution table.

Blunt gates

Under a 4-point edge or 50% confidence the answer is SIT OUT with the reason attached. A tool that always has an opinion is not a model.

FAQ

Is this an exchange?

No. Resolv is an analytics terminal. It reads public market data and stores your watchlist and logged positions locally in your browser. It never routes an order or holds funds.

Is the data live?

Opened as a file, the page calls the public Polymarket API and the GDELT news wire directly from your browser and shows them next to a labelled reference snapshot. Kalshi refuses requests from browsers, so Kalshi rows stay on the snapshot until you open Resolv through Start Resolv.command. Every source shows its state and age; anything unreachable is shown as offline rather than faked.

Are the estimates predictions?

On the sample contracts they are deterministic analytical outputs from documented factors: the exchange price, option maths where a tradable underlying exists, mapped news, liquidity and resolution ambiguity. Live quotes get an estimate only from a trained model that beat the market price in validation, and even then live calls stay on hold. Not guaranteed outcomes, not financial advice.

Does Resolv have a track record?

Not yet, and the terminal does not claim one. The pipeline records point-in-time quotes and every model forecast in a forward ledger and scores them as markets settle. The Model view shows that count as it grows, and no accuracy figure is shown until enough forecasts have settled to mean something.

Why Basic and Pro?

Everyday traders need clarity; desks need controls. Same model, different surface.
